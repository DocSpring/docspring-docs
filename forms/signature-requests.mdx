## Collect legally binding electronic signatures

Create a signature request to collect UETA and ESIGN compliant electronic signatures.

When you make an API request to fill out a PDF, you can specify that some fields must be filled in by certain people (including signature or initials fields). The PDF submission will be in a pending state until all of its signature requests have been completed. DocSpring emails each person a link to sign, or you can send them the links yourself, or embed the form on your own website. When everyone has filled in the form, the PDF will be generated, DocSpring emails it to the people who signed, and we can send your server a webhook notification.

Signature requests work with every template type. On a PDF template, signers fill in and sign the fields on the
template's pages. An HTML template, a Word template or a page design has no pages until its document is made, so
DocSpring makes the document first: see [HTML templates, Word templates and page designs](#html-templates-word-templates-and-page-designs).

To collect UETA and ESIGN compliant electronic signatures, DocSpring must record an audit trail that includes user authentication. This means that you need to send us some details about how and when your users have been authenticated.

## How Signature Requests Work

When DocSpring sends the emails (the default):

1. **Create a submission with signature requests**: the people who fill in and sign the document, and optionally the order they sign in, a message and reminders. See [Emails from DocSpring](/docs/api-guide/create-signature-requests/#emails-from-docspring).
2. **DocSpring emails each person** a link to its signing page, and reminds them if you asked it to.
3. **Each person signs** on the signing page.
4. **DocSpring generates the PDF** and emails it to the people who signed and to any CCs. Your server gets webhook notifications along the way.

When you send the links yourself or embed the form in your app, create the signature requests with `send_email: false`:

1. **Create a submission with signature requests**: include details about the people who will be filling out and signing the document, and how your app authenticated them.
2. **Send notification emails**: send your users links to the signing form and record the event.
3. **Request authentication tokens**: create a token when the user is ready to sign.
4. **Update authentication details**: make sure the authentication information is current.
5. **Render the signing form**: embed the form on your website or use the hosted signing page.
6. **Process the completed PDF**: receive webhook notifications when all signatures are collected.

This process ensures that DocSpring can record a complete audit trail of events, making all electronic signatures legally binding.

A signed PDF never has editable form fields, so nobody can change a value after it was signed. When a signature request asks someone to sign or initial, every field in the final PDF is static, even if the submission sets `editable: true` or the template generates editable PDFs ([Editable PDFs](/docs/api-guide/generate-pdfs/generate-pdfs-via-api/#editable-pdfs)). Signature requests that only collect values keep the PDF's editable form fields.

## HTML Templates, Word Templates and Page Designs

DocSpring makes the document when you create the signature requests, with the data you send. Signers read that
document and fill in its form fields. Once everyone has signed, DocSpring fills in their values on the same document,
without making it again, so each person signs what they read. It's always a PDF.

### Form fields

Signers fill in the template's form fields:

- HTML templates: the [`form_field`, `signature` and `initials` tags](/docs/html-templates/overview/#form-fields).
- Word templates: the [`form_field`, `signature` and `initials` tags](/docs/word-templates/tags/#form-fields).
- Page designs: the [form field elements](/docs/page-designer/layout-format/form-field/).

Each form field has a field on the template's field list, which DocSpring adds when you save the template. If you
delete the field from the list, submissions and previews fail with a message that names it.

A form field that no signer fills in prints its value from `data`.

A required form field must be in the document. When an `{% if %}` or a section's condition leaves one out for the data
you send, the submission fails with a message that names it, because its signer couldn't finish without it. Turn off the
field's **Required** setting in the field list when the data can leave it out. A tag's or element's `required` only
sets the field when it's added to the field list: after that, the field's **Required** setting decides, and changing
`required` doesn't change it. A field that a [show/hide rule](/docs/template-editor/conditional-logic/) hides for this
data doesn't count.

In a `{% for %}` loop or a repeating section, each item has its own field, and every item in the data needs its own when
it's required. Name the field after the loop's variable, as in `{% form_field room.initials, kind: initials %}` inside
`{% for room in rooms %}`. A signature request's `fields` can list them one at a time, `rooms/0/initials`, or all together,
`rooms/~/initials`. A list with no items has nothing to fill in.

A signer's field is always a value in the data, wherever the template reads it from: an item of a sorted or filtered
list (`{% assign by_floor = rooms | sort: "floor" %}`) is still that room, and a name set with a plain `assign` is still
that field. A value the template makes itself, with `capture` or a filter such as `upcase`, isn't a field, so no signer
can fill it in. A form field on an item of a sorted or filtered list needs the item to be an object
(`{% form_field room.initials %}`): for a list of plain values, loop over the list itself.

### Who fills in each field

Give the fields to signers with each signature request's `fields`, or with the template's
[signer roles](/docs/forms/signer-roles/): a tag's or element's `role` gives a form field to one of them, and `role:
sender` means `data` fills it in. A submission's only signature request can leave out `fields`, and gets every field
without a role that `data` doesn't fill in.

### The document doesn't change once it's made

Nothing that a signer fills in can change the rest of the document. A [show/hide
rule](/docs/template-editor/conditional-logic/) can read the data you send, and decide which form fields are drawn and
required, but creating a signature request is a 422 when a rule reads a field that a signer fills in on the document.

For the same reason, a signer can't fill in a form field whose value the text uses too, such as `{{ tenant_name }}`
beside `{% form_field tenant_name %}`: the text would stay empty next to the name they typed. Creating the signature
request is a 422 that names the field. Send the field in `data`, which fills in both, or use it in one place.

### Asking a signer for a value the document uses

The text of a document can use a value outside a form field, such as `{{ address }}`. That value has to be there when
the document is made, so send it in `data`, or use a form field instead.

The signer who signs first can also be asked for it, by listing the field in their `fields`. That's a submission's only
signer, or with `signing_order: sequential`, the only signer in the first `signing_step`. Listing it for anyone else is
a 422.

1. When they open their link, the signing page asks for those values, on a form with only those fields.
2. DocSpring makes the document with their answers, usually in a few seconds. Until then, nobody can open it.
3. They read the document and sign it. The **Change answers** button takes them back to the form, and the document is
   made again with their new answers. It can be made 5 times in all, and never once someone has signed.

A date they answer reaches the template as `YYYY-MM-DD`. To print it another way, use the `date` filter:
`{{ start_date | date: "%B %-d, %Y" }}`.

### When the document is ready

Creating the signature requests returns straight away, and DocSpring makes the document in the background, usually in a
few seconds. Until it's ready, a signing link or an embedded signing form shows that the document is being prepared and
opens it once it's there, and DocSpring sends its emails to signers only then.

If the document can't be made (a Liquid error, a required form field that the data leaves out, one of the limits
below), the submission's `state` is `error`, its `error_message` says why, and the
[`submission.failed`](/docs/webhooks/events/#submissions) webhook is sent. Nobody can sign it: fix the template or the
data and create a new submission.

### Limits

- The document can have up to 100 pages and 1,000 form fields for signers.
- Each form field is in the document once. One in a header, a footer or a table's repeated header row would be on
  every page, and one taller than the space left on its page would be split in two: both fail the submission with a
  message that names the field. Signature, initials and date signed fields can't go in a page design's header or footer.
- Signers fill in text, dates, numbers, check boxes, radio buttons, drop-down lists, signatures and initials. They
  can't fill in image fields.
- The signed document is always a PDF that can't be edited. A Word template's `output_format` of `docx` or `odt` and
  `editable: true` are refused with signature requests.

## Declining to Sign

A signer can choose not to sign by clicking the **Decline** button in the form header. The button is shown on the signing page that a [signature request token's](/docs/api/#tag/signature-requests/post/signature_requests/{signature_request_id}/tokens) `signature_request_url` opens (`data_request_url` in API v1), and when you embed the signing form with [Signature Requests JS](/docs/forms/js-libraries/signature-requests-js/) version 2.4.0 or above (older versions don't show it). In an embedded form, you can hide the button with the `showDecline: false` option, or rename it with the `declineButtonLabel` option (for example, `"Cancel - Do Not Sign"`).

Declining can't be undone:

- The signature request moves to the `declined` state, and a `decline_request` event is recorded with the signer's IP address and browser.
- The **entire submission is cancelled**. It moves to the `declined` state and **no PDF is generated**, even if other signers have already signed.
- DocSpring sends the `submission.declined` and `submission_data_request.declined` [webhook events](/docs/webhooks/events/). Webhooks created with API v2 receive the second one as `signature_request.declined`.

Once a submission has been declined, its remaining signature requests can no longer be opened or signed.

If you read submissions or signature requests with a DocSpring [API client](/docs/api-guide/install-api-client/), upgrade it to the latest version. Older versions of the Ruby, Python, PHP, Java and C# clients raise an error when they read the `declined` state.

## Credit Usage

The final live PDF costs its own generation credits plus **60 credits for
each signature request**. There is no separate charge for creating a
request, issuing access tokens, recording events, or generating previews.
Watermarked test submissions are free, and they're deleted after 30 days at the latest.

## Signature Request Components

### Authentication Requirements

To ensure legal compliance, signature requests should include:

- The user's name and email address. Both are optional, but we recommend
  including them because they are shown on the audit trail.
- Details about how and when the user was authenticated (`auth_type` is required)
- The fields that the user must fill out (including signature or initials fields)
- Optional metadata to save on the signature request

<Aside type="tip">
  While many authentication fields are optional, provide as much detail as you
  can. This helps DocSpring build a comprehensive audit trail and ensures
  electronic signatures will be legally binding if contested in court.
</Aside>

### Audit Trail

DocSpring automatically records a complete audit trail for each signature request, including:

- When DocSpring emailed the signing link or a reminder, and when that email bounced or was marked as spam.
- When you sent the signing link yourself, which you report through the [Create Signature Request Event](/docs/api/#tag/signature-requests/post/signature_requests/{signature_request_id}/events) API. The audit trail shows it as reported by your account.
- How and when each signer was authenticated, in a **Signer Authentication** section. These are the details you sent, shown as reported by your account. When a signer opens an email link, DocSpring records the time itself, and when the link was one DocSpring emailed, the section says DocSpring sent it to the signer's address.
- When the document was viewed
- When each signature was collected
- When the document was completed

The certificate also shows the document's **Document SHA-256**, the hash of the document before the certificate and any password were added, and an **Audit trail hash** of the certificate's details and events.

Keep the signed PDF, the audit trail and the hash manifest yourself: [Signing evidence and retention](/docs/forms/signing-evidence/) says what's deleted when a signed document expires and what stays. [How DocSpring signing works](/docs/forms/how-signing-works/) describes the evidence and how a document can be verified later.

Example audit trail log:

```
2024-01-01 12:00:00 - Reported by Acme Inc via API: sent a signing link by email to test@example.com, at 2024-01-01 11:59:30 UTC
2024-01-01 12:10:00 - Viewed by Test (test@example.com)
2024-01-01 12:20:00 - Signed by Test (test@example.com)
2024-01-01 12:20:00 - Document completed
```

## Your Logo and Brand Color

The signing page, DocSpring's emails to your signers and the audit trail show your account's logo, and the signing
page and emails use your brand color for their buttons. Set them in the **Logo** and **Brand color** fields on the
**Account** page. They show when your account is on a paid plan or has bought a credit pack. On the signing page, the
logo shows when the page is opened from its link, not when you embed the form on your own site.

## Authentication Tokens

Authentication tokens authorize a user to access and sign a specific signature request. Key characteristics:

- Its link opens the signing form for 24 hours (`api` tokens, the default) or 7 days (`email` tokens, for links you send by email). A form that opened in time keeps working for 7 more days, so the person can finish it later. After that, the page says the link has expired and to ask the sender for a new one.
- Can't be created once the signature request is finished (completed, declined, or expired). The API returns an error, and the same applies to updating the signature request or adding events to it.
- Should be requested immediately before showing the signing form
- Include an authenticated URL for the hosted signing page

## Mobile Support

When an embedded form is displayed on a mobile device, it will always be shown in a full-screen modal overlay. The user can zoom in to enlarge the document and fields. You should use the following `viewport` meta tag in your HTML `<head>`:

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1" />
```

<Aside type="caution">
  Do not use `maximum-scale=1` or `user-scalable=no` in this `viewport` meta
  tag, because this will prevent the user from zooming in.
</Aside>
